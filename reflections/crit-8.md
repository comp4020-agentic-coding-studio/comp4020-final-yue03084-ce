# Crit 8 reflection

The moment it came alive was the first time I clicked on a part that had been
swapped. I then had the agent show the right answer after five wrong tries
([b21a9e3](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-yue03084-ce/commit/b21a9e3)),
and that felt close to the game I want to make.

But my first picture of it was too simple. The scene has far too many details
to turn all of them into pixel art. The parts themselves generated well, but
they couldn't be put together the way the real room is laid out. If the scene
doesn't match the stills from the show, the game doesn't work.

I haven't decided what to do about this yet. If I really wanted to, I could
probably find better tools or skills to get a better result. But the amount of
detail also showed me that this game isn't as simple or as fun as I imagined:
the barrier to playing it is too high. That's something I could only find out
by actually building it. In future projects I might talk an idea through in
more detail before I start, though some trial and error is always part of it.

I was in a hurry and didn't handle the Fly token carefully, so it ended up in
the agent conversation. I've since replaced it. It's made me much more careful
with keys like this.
